00 — Start here

00Don't panic

If this is your first time inside a real codebase, here is the single most important thing to know: you are not expected to understand all of it at once. This document is a map, not a test.

The project is called HMD AGRO. It is software for running a dairy farm — tracking cows, the milk they give, when they get pregnant and give birth, what they eat, the medicines they receive, and the money that flows in and out. The people who use it are farm managers and workers, not programmers.

You'll meet a lot of French words (vache = cow, lait = milk, traite = a milking, vêlage = calving). The farm is in a French-speaking region, so the developers named everything in French. Don't fight it — there's a full glossary at the end, and once a few words click, the rest follow.

How to read this manual The order is deliberate. §01 teaches the framework (Frappe) — the rules of the world — because nothing else makes sense without it. §02 shows the farm and the app from 10,000 ft. §03 is the spine: a clickable map between the design diagram (the MCD you were handed) and the actual code. §04–08 drill into each part. §09–10 are your practical first-week kit.

01 — The framework

01What on earth is Frappe?

Before a single line of HMD code makes sense, you need to know the world it lives in. That world is Frappe, and its big sibling ERPNext.

Frappe is a web framework written in Python. ERPNext is a big business application (accounting, inventory, sales…) built on top of Frappe. HMD AGRO is a third layer: a custom app built on top of both, adding everything specific to a dairy farm.

Analogy Think of a kitchen. Frappe is the building, plumbing, and power. ERPNext is the fully-stocked commercial kitchen installed inside. HMD AGRO is the special menu and the few custom appliances this particular dairy needed. You're here to learn the custom menu — but you must respect the plumbing.

The five words you must own

🧱 DocType

The heart of Frappe. A DocType is one "kind of thing" — like Animal or Traite. Defining one (in a JSON file) automatically gives you a database table, a data-entry form, a list view, and a REST API. You describe the fields once; Frappe builds everything around them.

🏛️ Site & Bench

A bench is the developer's command-line toolbox and the folder holding everything. A site is one running instance (one farm's database + files). One bench can host many sites. You run things with bench … commands.

🪝 Hooks

A file called hooks.py is the app's registration form. It tells Frappe "run this function every day", "load this CSS", "ship these settings". Frappe reads it to know how to plug your app in. We dissect HMD's in §05.

📦 Fixtures

Fixtures are database records shipped with the code (not typed in by a user) — e.g. custom fields and the app's home screen. They're re-loaded automatically on install/upgrade so every farm starts identical.

Anatomy of one DocType folder

Almost everything in HMD is a DocType, and they all follow the same shape. Open hmd_agro/hmd_agro/doctype/animal/ and you'll see:

# doctype/animal/ — the five files you'll meet again and again
animal.json        # the SCHEMA: every field, its type, links, options. The source of truth.
animal.py          # the CONTROLLER: Python that runs on save/submit — the business rules.
animal.js          # the FORM SCRIPT: JavaScript that runs in the browser form (buttons, live tweaks).
animal_list.js     # tweaks to the LIST view (colours, indicators).
test_animal.py     # automated tests that prove the controller behaves.
Field types you'll see in the JSON Data = short text · Select = a dropdown/enum · Check = a yes/no box · Int/Float/Currency = numbers · Date/Datetime = time · Link = a pointer to another DocType (this is how a Pesee knows which Animal it belongs to — a foreign key) · Table = rows of a child DocType embedded inside a parent (like line-items on an invoice).
Analogy — DocType = a super-spreadsheet A DocType is like a spreadsheet tab that grew up: each column is a typed field, each row is a record — but it also comes with a real form, validation rules, permissions, an audit trail, and an API. A Link field is a column whose value must be a real row in another tab.

The lifecycle methods (where the logic lives)

Inside *.py controllers you'll repeatedly see methods with special names. Frappe calls them for you at the right moment — you never call them yourself:

MethodWhen Frappe runs it
validateRight before saving — the place to check data and compute derived fields. The workhorse.
before_save / before_insertJust before writing to the database.
on_updateAfter a successful save — e.g. to update a related record.
on_submit / on_cancelFor "submittable" documents that become locked/official (like a finalised milking).
on_trashBefore deletion — used here to clean up linked records (see cascade-delete tests in §08).

That's the whole framework in a nutshell. Everything below is just HMD's particular cows, milk, and rules expressed in these primitives.

02 — Orientation

02The farm, in one breath

A dairy farm is a loop. Understanding the loop makes the whole codebase fall into place.

A cow (Animal) lives in a pen (Lot) inside a barn (Batiment). To give milk, she must first have a calf — so she is inseminated (Insemination) with bull semen (Semence), and ~9 months later she calves (Velage). Calving starts a Lactation — a milk-producing period — during which she is milked two or three times a day, each milking recorded as a Traite. She eats a measured recipe (Ration made of Aliment ingredients), occasionally needs medical care (Traitement with a Medicament), gets weighed (Pesee), and the system raises Alertes when something looks wrong. Feed and medicine are drawn from stock, which costs money; milk is sold, which earns it.

Housing

Where animals live: Batiment → Lot, plus the allotement history of moving animals between pens.

Animal & health

The animal's identity card, weighings, body-condition, abortions, and veterinary treatments.

Reproduction & milk

Bulls, semen, insemination, calving, lactation curves, and the daily milkings that drive the whole business.

Products & stock

Feed, medicine and semen as inventory items — where HMD plugs into ERPNext's stock engine.

Feed recipes

Ration and its Composition lines — what each group of cows is fed each day.

Costs & sales

The money layer — costs of feed/care/equipment and the sale of milk and animals.

09 — Practical

09Your first week

  1. Get it runningFrom a bench, install the app onto a fresh site:
    bench get-app <repo-url> --branch develop then bench install-app hmd_agro. Then bench start and log into the Desk.
  2. Find the front doorIn the Desk, open the HMD AGRO workspace (the app's home screen — defined in workspace/hmd_agro/). Everything links from there.
  3. Open one AnimalCreate or open an Animal record. Watch the fields from §04 appear. Edit it, save, and read animal.py to see what validate just did.
  4. Follow one milkingRecord a Traite and trace how it feeds the Lactation curve and the daily dashboard numbers.
  5. Read before you writeBefore changing any controller, run its test (bench --site … run-tests --module …). If it's green, you have a safety net; if you break it, you'll know.
Two beginner traps 1. Don't hand-edit the database or the .json schema casually — change fields through the Desk's Customize Form / the DocType editor so Frappe stays consistent. 2. Everything is in French; a field called statut is "status", not a typo. Keep the glossary open.
